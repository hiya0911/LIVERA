Master Practitioner Spotlight
Oral Ballads of Pingla

“If you only paint the cloth, it is dead paper. When you sing, the painting breathes.”

The 38-year odyssey of Gurupada Chitrakar, keeping the Manasa Mangal scrolls alive through song in Paschim Medinipur.

In Naya village, every child learns how to grind marigold petals with wood-apple resin before they learn to write. When the monsoon brings river floods, the Chitrakars do not just seek shelter; they sing ancient warnings of river serpents and ecological balance passed down across three centuries.

Gurupada Chitrakar

Systemic Cultural Architecture

Why LIVERA? The 4 Core Pillars

Cultural knowledge disappears when left unrecorded; practitioners starve when divorced from modern discovery. LIVERA bridges preservation and sustainable cultural livelihoods.

Pillar 1

PRESERVE

Digital encoding of vulnerable oral histories, songs, and indigenous techniques with contributor consent, rights attribution, and community ownership.

Pillar 2

DISCOVER

Interactive geographic mapping and structured taxonomy of living traditions, enabling travelers, scholars, and youth to explore genuine culture.

Pillar 3

CONNECT

Direct peer-to-peer apprenticeship pathways connecting verified master craftspeople with learners, institutions, and research fellows.

Pillar 4

SUSTAIN

Cultural Passports with transparent provenance tracking, establishing fair compensation, intellectual protection, and institutional backing.

Educational Ethics & Transmission Disclaimer

LIVERA’s learning pathways are designed for cultural appreciation, transmission of knowledge, and building respect for traditional practitioners. Completion of these modules does not confer master artisan accreditation or grant permission to commercialize sacred community designs without direct practitioner partnership.
